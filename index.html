1. Biot-Savart & Far-Field Magnetic Dipole Stray Leakage

At inductive charging frequencies ($65 - 150\,\text{kHz}$), the transmitter pad forms an alternating magnetic dipole moment $\mathbf{m}(t) = I_{\text{tx}} N A \hat{\mathbf{z}} \cos(\omega t)$. In terrestrial environments, conductive subsoils and damp ground planes induce eddy currents that damp long-range magnetic fields. On the Moon, dry vacuum and non-conductive anorthositic lunar regolith ($\sigma \approx 10^{-13}\,\text{S/m}$, $\mu_r \approx 1.000$) create zero skin-effect damping, allowing stray fields to propagate according to pure dipole vacuum falloff:

$$\mathbf{B}_{\text{stray}}(r, \theta) = \frac{\mu_0 m_0}{4\pi r^3} \left( 2\cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{r}} + \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \right) + \sum_{n=3,5,7} \frac{\mu_0 I_n A_n}{4\pi r^3}\cos(n\omega t + \phi_n)$$

Where $r$ is the radial distance to the scientific lander, $\theta$ is the polar angle relative to the coil normal, and $I_n$ represents high-order odd harmonics generated by the Gallium Nitride (GaN) high-speed switching bridge.

2. Active Vector Superposition via Concentric Opposing Coils

To suppress stray magnetic flux without degrading the mutual coupling $M_{\text{tx-rx}}$ into the rover receiver coil, the system integrates a concentric peripheral cancellation coil $L_c$. The cancellation coil is driven in exact anti-phase ($\Delta \phi = 180^\circ$) with an amplitude ratio dynamically scaled by the magnetic state observer:

$$\mathbf{B}_{\text{net}}(r) = \mathbf{B}_{\text{tx}}(r) + \mathbf{B}_{\text{cancel}}(r) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}\left[ N_{\text{tx}} I_{\text{tx}} A_{\text{tx}} - N_c I_c(t) A_c \right] \approx 0 \quad (\text{for } r \gg R_{\text{coil}})$$

Because near-field coupling scales with local geometry ($1/(z^2+a^2)^{3/2}$), the receiver coil docked at distance $z=10\,\text{cm}$ couples predominantly with $L_{\text{tx}}$, retaining an efficiency $\eta > 93.4\%$, while far-field flux at $r > 10\,\text{m}$ sums destructively, achieving an attenuation factor $\ge 35\,\text{dB}$.

3. Kalman State Inversion & Adaptive Frequency Hopping

Scientific instruments (such as fluxgate magnetometers and broadband seismometers) exhibit narrow-band, highly resonant vulnerability corridors. The software maintains a continuous real-time state observer estimating the transfer function $H(f) = B_{\text{sensor}}(f)/I_{\text{tx}}(f)$. If harmonic leakage infringes upon a science band, the controller executes a rapid zero-voltage-switching (ZVS) frequency hop:

$$f_{\text{target}} = \arg \min_{f \in [65, 145\,\text{kHz}]} \left\{ \sum_{k=1}^7 w_k \cdot \left| B(k \cdot f) \right| + \lambda \left( 1 - \eta_{\text{transfer}}(f) \right) \right\}$$

The dual-objective cost function penalizes both instrument interference weights ($w_k$) and power transfer degradation ($\lambda$). The frequency transition executes in $< 1.2\,\text{ms}$, seamlessly maintaining charging continuity.
